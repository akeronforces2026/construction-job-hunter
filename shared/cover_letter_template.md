# Student Cover Letter Template — STAR-based

One page. 300–400 words (slightly shorter than mid-career letters). British English. Confident but appropriate to a student/graduate writer — neither falsely senior nor apologetic.

## Structure

```
[Student's name]
[Email] · [Phone] · [City]

[Date — DD Month YYYY]

[Recruiter / Hiring Manager name if findable, otherwise "Hiring Manager" or "[Company] Recruitment Team"]
[Company]
[Address — line 1]
[Address — line 2]

Dear [Name / Hiring Manager],

Re: [Exact job title] — [Reference number if quoted]

[Paragraph 1 — Opening, 40–60 words]
[Paragraph 2 — STAR #1, 70–100 words]
[Paragraph 3 — STAR #2, 70–100 words]
[Paragraph 4 — Why this company, 40–60 words]
[Paragraph 5 — Close, 30–50 words]

Yours sincerely,
[Student's name]
```

## Paragraph 1 — Opening

Formula: state which role you're applying for, where you saw it, your current course and university, and one-sentence statement of motivation.

**Example (placeholder — replace details with your own):**

> I am writing to apply for the [Construction Management Graduate Scheme — Reference GS2026] advertised on [Prospects on 12 October]. I am a final-year [BSc Construction Management] student at [University Name], graduating in [July 2026], and I am seeking a graduate role with a tier 1 contractor where I can begin a structured route to chartered status whilst contributing to large-scale London projects.

## Paragraph 2 — STAR #1

Pick your strongest, most JD-relevant achievement. For students this is often a substantial university project, a placement, or a leadership role.

**Pattern:**
> [Situation — 1–2 sentences setting up the project/role/context]. [Task — 1 sentence on what was required]. [Action — 2 sentences on what you specifically did, naming any methods or tools the JD names]. [Result — 1 sentence with quantified outcome].

**Example (placeholder):**

> During my second-year integrated design module, I led a team of five students on a simulated £4 million mixed-use refurbishment scheme in Manchester. As project coordinator, I was responsible for sequencing the team's design contributions, maintaining the project programme in MS Project, and presenting our final design to a panel of three industry assessors from [a regional contractor]. I introduced a shared task board to replace ad-hoc messaging, ran weekly 30-minute coordination meetings, and personally produced our project programme and risk register. We achieved 81% — the highest in our cohort of 27 teams — and won the module's "Best Project Coordination" award.

## Paragraph 3 — STAR #2

Different competency to paragraph 2. If para 2 was leadership/project, paragraph 3 might cover technical skills, a placement, or a soft-skills evidencing experience like part-time work or volunteering.

**Example (placeholder):**

> Over the summer of 2024 I completed a 4-week placement with [Regional Contractor] on a £6M residential refurbishment in Birmingham. I shadowed the Site Manager on daily site walks, documented snags in PlanRadar, and drafted two formal RFIs to the design team that were sent without amendment. I also attended weekly subcontractor coordination meetings, where I observed how interface clashes between the M&E and dry-lining packages were resolved through scheduling adjustments. I received a written reference from the Site Manager confirming readiness for a structured placement role.

## Paragraph 4 — Why This Company

Specific reference to the company's projects, sectors, values, or graduate development. This is where many student cover letters fall flat — generic flattery ("your prestigious organisation") gets your CV binned. Show you've done research.

**Example (placeholder):**

> [Company]'s recent delivery of [specific named project — e.g., the £180M Battersea Phase 4 fit-out] caught my attention particularly because it combines the high-rise residential and complex MEP coordination challenges I most want to develop in. Your published commitment to chartered-status sponsorship for graduates and your structured rotational scheme also align directly with my career plan to pursue MCIOB within the first three years.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:**
> ❌ "I have always been impressed by [Company]'s reputation for excellence and innovation." (This says nothing.)

## Paragraph 5 — Close

**Example:**

> I am available for assessment from [date — e.g., the start of December once my final exams conclude] and would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my academic record, project leadership, and recent placement experience could contribute to your 2026 graduate intake. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> [Name]

## Handling Genuine Gaps Honestly

Insert a brief paragraph (40–80 words) between paragraphs 3 and 4 only if there's a genuine gap that the JD will obviously notice.

**For a missing certification (e.g., CSCS):**
> I do not yet hold a CSCS card, however I have booked the green CSCS labourer test for [date — within 2 weeks] and will hold the card before any site visit. I plan to upgrade to the relevant student/graduate card during my first month if successful.

**For a missing driving licence (when role is site-based):**
> I am working towards my UK driving licence with my test scheduled for [month], and I am confident I will hold a full licence before any role start date. In the interim I am happy to use public transport or arrange shared transport with colleagues.

**For a predicted grade gap:**
Don't address directly unless the JD specifies a minimum your prediction is below. If asked at interview, be honest about which modules pulled the average down and what you've done since.

## Anti-Patterns Specific to Student Letters

- ❌ Starting with "As a passionate and motivated student..." — every cover letter ever written starts like this. Skip it.
- ❌ Listing every society and society role you've ever held. Pick one or two relevant to the JD.
- ❌ Apologising for limited experience. You're a student; experience is expected to be limited. Demonstrate transferable skills instead.
- ❌ Repeating the CV verbatim. The cover letter must add narrative the CV's bullets can't carry — typically the "why this role, why this employer, why now" story.
- ❌ Word salad ("synergistic", "leverage", "ecosystem"). Plain professional English wins.
- ❌ Sending the same letter to every employer with name changed. Every cover letter must reference the specific company's projects or values in paragraph 4.
