# Interview Question Bank — Construction Grad Schemes / Placements

For each prep pack, pick 8–12 weighted toward what the JD emphasises. For each, draft a 4-line STAR skeleton from the student's actual experiences (uni projects, placements, part-time work, extracurriculars, volunteering). Never invent.

## Category A — Motivation & Fit

1. Why construction management?
2. Why this company specifically? (You should have 2–3 specific reasons — projects, values, training structure)
3. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
4. What attracts you to a tier 1 contractor over a tier 2 / regional firm? (Or vice versa)
5. Why graduate scheme rather than direct entry?
6. What three words would your project group describe you as?
7. What's the most recent construction news story that interested you and why?
8. Have you applied elsewhere? (Be honest — they expect yes; don't pretend they're your only choice)

## Category B — Behavioural / STAR (Most Important Category)

9. Tell me about a time you led a team.
10. Tell me about a time you had to deal with conflict in a team.
11. Tell me about a time you missed a deadline (or nearly did) and how you handled it.
12. Tell me about your proudest achievement so far.
13. Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly.
14. Tell me about a time you persuaded someone to your point of view.
15. Describe a problem you solved that didn't have an obvious answer.
16. Tell me about a time you had to manage your time across competing demands.
17. Tell me about a time you received critical feedback and how you used it.
18. Tell me about a time you took the initiative.
19. Describe a setback or failure and what you learned.

## Category C — Technical Awareness (Don't Over-Demand for Grads)

For grad schemes, technical depth isn't expected — broad awareness is. The interviewer wants to know you've done basic background reading.

20. What do you understand by CDM 2015? (Brief: PD/PC/Designer/Contractor duties — don't pretend to be expert)
21. What's the Building Safety Act 2022 and why does it matter for construction?
22. What's the difference between a JCT contract and an NEC contract? (Brief: JCT is risk-allocating; NEC is collaborative/programme-driven)
23. What's BREEAM and why do clients care?
24. Walk me through how a building gets from idea to handover at a high level.
25. What's a critical path on a construction programme?
26. What does "value engineering" mean?
27. What's the difference between RIBA Stages 0 and 7?
28. What do you know about MMC / modern methods of construction?
29. What's BIM and why does it matter?

## Category D — Site & Practical Awareness

30. Have you been on a live construction site? Tell me about what you observed.
31. What PPE is required on a UK site?
32. What's a CSCS card and which one will you need?
33. What's a toolbox talk?
34. What's the difference between a Site Manager and a Project Manager?

## Category E — Curveballs / Commercial Awareness

35. If our share price dropped 20% tomorrow, what would you assume happened?
36. How would you explain what your dissertation is about to a 10-year-old?
37. If you were given £1M to improve UK construction, what would you spend it on?
38. What's the biggest challenge facing the UK construction industry right now? (Skills shortage, productivity, sustainability/Net Zero, post-Grenfell regulation, building safety)
39. Tell me something about yourself that's not on your CV.

## Category F — Group Exercises (Assessment Centre Tips)

When facing a group exercise:

- **Take a defined role early** — timekeeper, scribe, ideas summariser. Don't try to dominate; assessors mark down domination.
- **Reference others by name** — "Building on Sarah's point..." shows listening.
- **Agree a structure first** — spend 2 minutes agreeing how you'll attack the problem before brainstorming.
- **Watch the time** — if the group is going off-piste, gently redirect: "We've got 8 minutes left and we still need to cover X."
- **Bring quiet members in** — "We haven't heard from James yet — what do you think?" This is leadership without dominance.
- **Have a view** — silence is marked down hardest. Even if you're wrong, contributing is better than not.
- **Summarise at the end** — if the group hasn't appointed someone to summarise, volunteer.

## Category G — Numerical / Verbal Tests

Most tier 1 grad schemes use online tests before interview. Common formats:

- **Numerical reasoning:** percentages, ratios, reading data from graphs/tables, currency conversions, basic algebra. Practise on practiceaptitudetests.com or assessmentday.co.uk (both have free samples).
- **Verbal reasoning:** True / False / Cannot Say based on a passage. Read carefully — the answer must come from the passage, not from outside knowledge.
- **Situational judgement tests:** rank or pick the most/least appropriate response. The "right" answer is usually the one that follows process and respects others, not the one that "gets the job done fastest".
- **Inductive / logical reasoning:** pattern-recognition with shapes. Less common for construction but possible.

Spend 5–10 hours on practice tests before the real one. Improvement curves are steep at first.

## Questions to Ask Them (Always Include 5–8)

When the interviewer says "Do you have any questions for us?", saying "no" is a soft mark against you. Have these ready:

- What does success in the first 12 months of the graduate scheme look like?
- How is the rotation between sites / departments structured?
- What support is there for chartered status (MCIOB, MAPM, MRICS) during and after the scheme?
- What kind of projects am I likely to be exposed to in the first year?
- What's the appraisal and feedback structure?
- What's the typical career path 3–5 years after completing the scheme?
- What surprised you most about the company when you joined?
- Is there anything about my application or this conversation you'd like me to expand on?

Avoid asking about salary or holidays at first interview unless the interviewer raises it.

## STAR Skeleton Generator

For each behavioural question, populate this skeleton from the student's CV profile:

```
Q: [Question]
S (Situation): [Project / role / context — 1 sentence]
T (Task): [What was required of you — 1 sentence]
A (Action): [3 specific things you did — bullets]
R (Result): [Outcome, quantified where possible — 1 sentence]
```

Pick the experience that best fits the question's competency. Cycle through different experiences across questions to avoid repeating the same story 4 times — assessors mark this down.

If the student genuinely doesn't have an experience matching a behavioural question, say so honestly: "I haven't yet been in that exact situation. The closest I've come is [adjacent example] — would you like me to talk through that?" This is much better than fabrication.
