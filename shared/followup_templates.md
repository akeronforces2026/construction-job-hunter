# Follow-up Email Templates — Student Edition

Always log follow-up drafts into the application's Notes column at logging time so the student can copy when the dates come.

## +10 Days — Light Touch

**Subject:** Application — [Job Title] — [Student Name]

Dear [Hiring Manager / Recruiter Name],

I hope you are well. I wanted to briefly check in on my application for the [Job Title] role with [Company], submitted on [date].

I remain very interested in the opportunity and would welcome any update on the recruitment timeline, as well as the chance to provide any further information that would assist your shortlisting.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,
[Student Name]
[Phone]

---

## +21 Days — Slightly Firmer, Adds One Value Point

**Subject:** Re: Application — [Job Title] — [Student Name]

Dear [Hiring Manager / Recruiter Name],

Following up on my application for the [Job Title] role submitted on [original date], I wanted to reiterate my strong interest and share one additional point that may be relevant.

Since my initial application I have [completed a CSCS green card test / attended a CIOB student talk on the Building Safety Act / passed my [module] with [grade] / something genuine and recent], which strengthens my preparation for the role.

I appreciate that recruitment timelines for graduate schemes can be lengthy, and I would be grateful for any indication of where my application currently sits. I am happy to provide any additional information.

Kind regards,
[Student Name]

---

## After Online Test — Brief Acknowledgement

**Subject:** Online assessment completed — [Job Title] — [Student Name]

Dear [Recruiter Name],

I wanted to confirm that I have today completed the online assessment for the [Job Title] role.

Thank you for the opportunity to progress to this stage. I look forward to hearing the outcome and to any next steps in the process.

Kind regards,
[Student Name]

## After Video Interview — Same-Day Thank You

**Subject:** Thank you — [Job Title] video interview — [Student Name]

Dear [Interviewer Name],

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me [today / yesterday] regarding the [Job Title] role. I particularly enjoyed the discussion around [one specific topic from the interview — e.g., your graduate rotation between site and pre-construction, or your recent project at X].

The conversation reinforced my interest in joining [Company]'s 2026 graduate intake, and I am confident the experience I described — leading my second-year integrated design project and completing a summer placement on a live refurbishment scheme — would translate well into your structured graduate route.

If any additional information would help your decision, please do let me know.

Kind regards,
[Student Name]

---

## Polite Decline (when juggling multiple offers)

**Subject:** [Job Title] — Withdrawal of Application

Dear [Recruiter Name],

Thank you very much for the offer of the [Job Title] role at [Company], and for the time you and the team invested in my application.

After careful consideration I have accepted an offer that, on balance, fits my career plans more closely at this stage. I wanted to let you know promptly out of respect for your timeline.

I have a great deal of respect for [Company] and the way the recruitment process was handled, and I would welcome the chance to be considered for future opportunities.

Kind regards,
[Student Name]

---

## Rules

1. Never send follow-ups more than twice for the same application. After +21 days with no reply, mark as Ghosted in the sheet and move on.
2. Always personalise the bracketed sections — never send a follow-up that still has placeholders.
3. Tone: confident and professional, not pleading. Students often write apologetic-sounding emails — resist this.
4. If the original application was through a graduate platform like Prospects or RateMyPlacement, the follow-up still goes to the company's named recruiter (find them on LinkedIn if not given), not to the platform.
5. For grad schemes, expect longer windows than mid-career applications. +10 and +21 days reflect this.
